# Afterglow (working title)

A premium, Apple-level mobile app for discovering live DJ and festival performances. It combines a TikTok-style feed of live-set clips, behavior-driven artist recommendations, festival lineups and an interactive "My Sound" artist bubble map. The long-term headline feature is a personalized festival schedule built from the user's taste.

Expo / React Native / TypeScript. Also read @AGENTS.md (Expo's own agent rules: use `npx expo install`, Expo Router, current SDK docs rather than memory).

Built so far:
- **Discover**: full-screen vertical feed, one clip per page; tap to pause, double-tap to like, Like/Save/Skip rail, progress bar, haptics. Clips with a `Clip.source` play a section of an official YouTube upload in YouTube's own embedded player (`react-native-webview`), with the video on top and the clip info and Like/Save/Skip below it, never over the player. Clips without one, or whose video can't play, show the placeholder "stage scene" drawn in code (beams, beat-synced glow, crowd). The 10 YouTube sources in `data/mock/clips.ts` were verified by the user on 2026-09-28 (public, official channel, right artist, plays embedded).
- **Discover playback (Milestone 6.5)**: only the on-screen clip plays, and only while Discover is the visible screen and the app is in the foreground (another tab, a pushed page, or a backgrounded app pauses video and stage alike; coming back resumes unless you paused). Rules are pure in `features/discover/playback.ts` and tested. The feed only talks to `ClipPlayer` (still from the video while loading, fades into the player once it plays; picks the player per provider); YouTube details stay in `YouTubePlayer`/`youtube.ts`/`media.ts`, so a native video source can slot in later. A vertical swipe that starts on the video moves the feed (`feedPager.ts`). Optional vertical footage: `source.vertical: true` (official Shorts) gets a tall 9:16 player with the rail beside it (`videoLayout.ts`); no Shorts are in the feed yet. `ClipPlayer.onPlayed` reports only live, actually-playing seconds: any watch-time signal must use it and nothing else.
- **My Sound**: bubble map. Top match pinned at centre, others placed by sound direction and distance by match, label-aware collision (no overlaps), pan with momentum, pinch zoom, double-tap recenter, bubbles trail and wobble when panned, reshape animation when taste changes, glass artist sheet.
- **Festivals** tab, festival pages (lineup sorted For you / Billing, set times when published, optional lineup poster with full-screen zoom viewer).
- **Artist** pages: match, sound tags, where they play, clips (open in Discover), similar artists.
- **Taste engine** v1 and **persistent reactions** (AsyncStorage).
- **Watch behaviour (Milestone 7)**: how a real clip was watched is a quiet, passive taste signal. `features/taste/watch.ts` (pure) keeps one session per visit to a clip, fed only by `ClipPlayer.onPlayed` spans, and tracks *coverage* (merged parts of the clip), so repeated reports or seeking back never count the same seconds twice. On leaving the clip: ~all of it covered = `through`, a full pass plus half again = `replayed`, 1 to 4s played then swiped away = `quick`. Loading, hidden, background or stage-placeholder playback gives nothing; leaving Discover or opening the artist mid-clip excuses a short watch. Per clip the strongest watch is kept (through twice on separate visits = replayed; never beyond). Engine weights: through 0.3, replayed 0.5, quick -0.2 (like 1, save 1.3, skip -0.8); a skip cancels the watch on that clip, a like/save cancels a quick swipe, and all passive signal per artist is capped to [-0.3, 0.6], below one like or skip. Watches spill over to similar sounds like likes. My Sound's subtitle counts watched clips.
- **Discover feed ranking (Milestone 8)**: `features/recommend/feed.ts` (pure, deterministic, no randomness) orders the feed from the live taste (which already carries likes/saves/skips/watching and spill-over) plus what only the feed knows. Each artist is in one lane: `personal` (match >= 0.75), `explore` (below that, or unknown, but sound-similar >= 0.3 to one of your top 3 artists), `discover` (a different sound, or no signal at all; unknown artists first). Lanes take turns in a fixed pattern P P E P E D (empty explore turns fall back to personal). Every artist gets one clip before anyone's second, never the same artist twice in a row (feasibility guard at the tail). Blocks: fresh clips, then clips of artists you turned down (more skips than likes+saves), then clips seen in the last 6h (seen longest ago first), then skipped clips. Each item carries a `Reason`; `recommend/copy.ts` turns it into a quiet note after the match ("Close to ILLENIUM", "A different sound", "New to you"; strong matches get none). `useRankedFeed` keeps the clip on screen and the next one fixed and re-ranks the rest as you react. Viewing history (`state/seen.tsx`, clip -> last seen ms, bounded to 500) is marked when a clip is on screen while the feed is live; it never feeds the taste engine.
- **Festival discovery loop (Milestone 9)**: Discover is always focused on one festival (default: the first with clips) and only shows artists on its lineup. Its header ("Solstice 2026 ›", "Live sets from the lineup · dates") opens the festival page. Under each clip, the artist's place there (set time, or "On the lineup, set time to come") is a link to the festival page, with a small star if you starred them there. The artist button opens the artist in that festival's context (must-see star works). Festival pages have a "Discover the lineup" pill (`/?festival=<id>`) that refocuses Discover; jumping to a clip (`/?clip=<id>`) moves the focus if that artist doesn't play the current festival, and artist pages opened from a festival pass it along. Relationships are pure in `features/festivals/links.ts` (playsAt, lineupClips, defaultFocus, focusForClip, nextFocus, festivalLine, lineupReach) and tested.
- **Data architecture (Milestone 10)**: three layers. *Data* (`src/data`): plain-data models in `models.ts` (Artist, Festival, SetTime/FestivalSet, FestivalLineup, FestivalPhase, Clip, ClipSource, ArtistSimilarity, CatalogSnapshot, CatalogSource), mock records in `data/mock/` (the only mock data; `MOCK_SNAPSHOT`, `MOCK_CATALOG`, `mockSource`), `validate.ts` (cleans a snapshot and lists what it dropped; the mock has no problems), `catalog.ts` (`createCatalog`: indexed lookups, `clipsOf`, `festivalsOf`, `lineupOf`, `phaseOf`; separately published lineups replace a festival's lineup/sets), `festival.ts` (setFor, stageName, festivalPhase). *Recommendation logic*: pure modules taking data as arguments (taste engine, watch, similarity, feed, plan, sound-map layout, festival links); `features/taste/similarity.ts` holds `soundSimilarity` and `similarityWith(provided)` (catalogue scores first, sound tags as fallback), passed through `useTaste().similarity` so every "similar sound" agrees. *Presentation*: screens and components read catalogue data only via `useCatalog()` (`state/catalog.tsx`, `CatalogProvider`, starts with the mock catalogue; given a `source` it loads, validates and swaps in). Golden snapshot tests (`data/__tests__/golden.test.ts`, run `npx jest --ci`) prove the mock data computes exactly what it did before; `architecture.test.ts` enforces the layer rules.
- **Catalogue files (Milestone 11)**: real festivals, artists, set times and clips come in as a JSON catalogue file (format v1, guide in `docs/catalogue.md`, working example `docs/catalogue-example.json`). `data/file.ts` (pure) parses it forgivingly (YouTube links of any kind incl. Shorts -> vertical, "25:00" times, `artist`/`stage` keys, https-only images, safe ids, clips under 3 min) and never throws, returning plain-language problems. `data/merge.ts`: `mergeSnapshots` (file records first, same id wins) and `buildCatalog` (mode `add` on top of the demo data or `replace`; a file that leaves no festival with clips keeps the old data). `catalogue/afterglow.json` is bundled (`data/bundled.ts`, `BUILT_IN`; empty today). Optional `CATALOG_URL` (`config/data.ts`, https only, empty by default) points at a hosted static file: `data/remote.ts` `refreshCatalog` shows the saved copy (AsyncStorage, keyed to the link) then a fresh one, and never lets a broken, oversized or unreachable file replace good data. Problems go to the Expo terminal as a "Catalogue" warning in dev only. Discover falls back to the first festival with clips if its festival disappears.
- **Big lineups**: My Sound shows only artists the engine is informed about (prior or signal, incl. spill-over), best first, at most `MAP_MAX` = 40 (`artistsOnMap` in `sound-map/layout.ts`), so it grows as you react. "Your festival" shows the best `EXPLORE_SHOWN` = 8 explore picks plus "N more to explore in the full lineup below". Discover opens on the festival with the most clips (`defaultFocus`). An artist may have no `sound` (shows as New; never reached by spill-over). EDC sound tags were set by Claude from general knowledge for 74 acts and left empty for 35; the user may correct them.
- **Artist ratings (Milestone 12)**: rate an artist without a clip, "Love their sound" or "Not for me" (`features/taste/ratings.ts`, pure; `state/ratings.tsx`, AsyncStorage `artistRatings.v1`). The engine counts love like a save (1.3) and pass like a skip (-0.8), with spill-over; `computeTaste(..., similarity, ratings)`, empty ratings change nothing. The feed treats a pass as turning the artist down (cooled) and never penalises a love. Artist pages have `RateArtist` pills. Festival pages have a "Tune your lineup" pill opening `tune/[id]` (modal): lineup artists one at a time in billing order minus rated ones (`features/festivals/tune.ts`, queue fixed at start), Love / Not for me / Don't know them, undo, progress, a finished state; card slides out toward the answer (off with reduced motion). My Sound's subtitle counts rated artists (`useTaste().rated`).
- **Screen smoke tests**: `src/app/__tests__/screens.test.tsx` renders the real routes, providers and catalogue with `expo-router/testing-library` (`renderRouter`) and exercises Festivals, EDC's page, Solstice's timetable, the Tune flow (love, undo, skip, pass), artist rating, My Sound and Discover focus. Setup in `test/setup.ts` mocks AsyncStorage, haptics, the web view, worklets, and patches reanimated's mock at `react-native-reanimated/mock` (expo-router's testing library re-mocks reanimated with it). With @testing-library/react-native 14, `render` and `fireEvent` are async: always `await` them. The virtualized feed only renders clips near the top in tests (no scrolling). Discover clears handled `clip`/`festival` params with the screen's own `navigation.setParams`, which works on a cold-start link; the global `router.setParams` throws before the navigation container is ready.
- **Festival dates and set notes**: `Festival.startDate`/`endDate` ("YYYY-MM-DD"; catalogue keys `start`/`end`, checked as real days, end not before start) drive a countdown (`features/festivals/when.ts`, pure: "In N days" under 60, then months, "Tomorrow", "Happening now", "Over"; calendar days in local time) on the Festivals card and festival page, via `lib/useToday` (refreshes on foreground). `Festival.notes` (artist id -> short text; catalogue lineup entries `{ "artist", "note" }`; dropped for artists not on the lineup) shows after the set time via `setLine` in lineup rows, artist pages and under Discover clips. EDC has its dates and the poster's notes (10 sunset sets, SAN HOLO wholesome riddim set, SKULL MACHINE = Black Tiger Sex Machine x Kai Wachi).
- **I'm going**: festival pages have an "I'm going" / "Going" pill (`GoingPill`); `state/going.tsx` (AsyncStorage `going.v1`, planning only, never fed to the taste engine). The Festivals tab groups festivals (`groupFestivals` in `features/festivals/going.ts`, pure): "Going" first (in progress or soonest start first, then undated, then finished), then "More festivals" in catalogue order; no headings when you're going to nothing.
- **Share your plan**: a share glyph beside "Your festival" opens the system share sheet (`Share.share`, built into React Native) with `planText` (`features/plan/share.ts`, pure): before set times, Must see and Strong match lists; after, day by day ("11:40 PM  ILLENIUM, Main Stage", close calls as "or ...", clear/pinned clashes as "(clashes with ...)") plus "Set time to come"; ★ for starred picks; explore picks left out; "Made with Afterglow". Hidden when nothing is planned.
- **Your festival** (Milestone 5) on each festival page. Before set times: lineup ranked into Must see / Strong match / Explore from the live match. After set times: day-by-day timeline, clashes grouped with a suggestion (clear winner, close call, or your starred pick, plus how much of the other set you can still catch), explore picks as discoveries when you're free or as a quiet alternative. A star marks a personal must-see at that festival only (festivalId + artistId); it's a planning preference stored apart from reactions and never changes the match. Artist pages opened from a festival get a `festival` route param and star for that festival; opened elsewhere they show no star.

## Product principles

- **Visual standard is non-negotiable**: Apple-level polish, dark-first, minimal and premium, excellent typography and spacing, smooth purposeful motion, tasteful blur/glass, strong but meaningful haptics. No generic dashboard look, no cheap neon "EDM app" look. The accent is one restrained purple glow ("festival light at night"). Artist imagery should eventually drive per-artist color.
- **The bubble map is the core differentiator.** Bubble size = predicted affinity. Similar sounds cluster naturally. It should feel like a living musical universe, not a chart.
- **Recommendations are behavior-driven.** Scores come from what the user does: likes, saves and skips, plus quieter watch behaviour (watched through, replayed, quick swipe away), all with spill-over to similar sounds; later follows and other meaningful signals. Passive signals must never outweigh explicit ones. Never replace this with arbitrary static scores. `data/mock/tasteProfile.ts` is only the starting prior.
- **Festivals have two phases.** Lineups are known months ahead; set times arrive a week or two before. Every schedule feature must work gracefully in both.
- Copy is plain, sentence case, user-facing (no developer language in the UI).

## Architecture

```
src/
  app/                        Expo Router routes (file = screen)
    _layout.tsx               Root Stack + GestureHandlerRootView + Catalog/Reactions/Plan/Seen providers
    (tabs)/_layout.tsx        Tabs with custom GlassTabBar
    (tabs)/index.tsx          Discover
    (tabs)/sound.tsx          My Sound
    (tabs)/festivals.tsx      Festivals list
    artist/[id].tsx           Artist page (stack push)
    festival/[id].tsx         Festival page (stack push)
    poster/[id].tsx           Lineup poster viewer (full-screen modal)
    tune/[id].tsx             Tune your lineup: rate a festival's artists one at a time (modal)
  components/                 Shared UI: NightSky, GlassTabBar, GlassCircleButton, ArtistAvatar, ArtistRow, TabIcons
  features/discover/          DiscoverFeed, useRankedFeed, ClipCard, StageScene, ActionRail, icons, ClipPlayer (the one video surface), YouTubePlayer, useFeedLive, feedPager; pure: playback.ts (lifecycle, watch eligibility), media.ts (provider-neutral clip media), videoLayout.ts, youtube.ts (source checks, player page)
  features/sound-map/         layout.ts (pure layout engine), SoundMap, ArtistBubble, ArtistSheet
  features/taste/engine.ts    Pure taste engine: reactions -> TasteProfile (+ which scores rest on real signal)
  features/taste/watch.ts     Pure watch sessions: played spans -> quick / through / replayed, coverage-based, bounded
  features/festivals/         links.ts (pure festival/artist/clip relationships, Discover's festival focus), format.ts (formatTime, describeSet)
  features/taste/similarity.ts  soundSimilarity + similarityWith(provided)
  features/recommend/         feed.ts (pure: lanes, scoring, interleaving, seen history), copy.ts (reason notes)
  features/plan/              plan.ts (pure: tiers, schedule, clash verdicts), mustSees.ts (pure per-festival stars), copy.ts, YourFestival, MustSeeList, Timetable, MustSeeToggle
  state/reactions.tsx         Likes/saves/skips/watched context, persisted to AsyncStorage
  state/taste.ts              useTaste(): live profile + summary
  state/seen.tsx              When each clip was last on screen, persisted to AsyncStorage (feed ordering only)
  state/ratings.tsx           Artists rated directly (love / pass), persisted to AsyncStorage, fed to the taste engine
  state/going.tsx             Festivals you're going to, persisted to AsyncStorage (orders the Festivals tab; planning only)
  state/plan.tsx              Starred must-sees per festival, persisted to AsyncStorage (planning only, never fed to the taste engine)
  data/                       models.ts (domain types; types.ts re-exports), catalog.ts, validate.ts, festival.ts, file.ts (catalogue file format), merge.ts, remote.ts, bundled.ts, mock/ (the only mock data)
  config/data.ts              CATALOG_URL (hosted catalogue file, optional)
  state/catalog.tsx           CatalogProvider + useCatalog(): the only way screens reach catalogue data
  theme/tokens.ts             Color, type, spacing, radius, motion springs. Use these, never ad-hoc values.
  lib/haptics.ts              Haptic vocabulary (select, tap, soft, settle)
  config/app.ts               App name
catalogue/afterglow.json      The bundled catalogue file (real data goes here)
docs/catalogue.md             Plain-language guide to the catalogue file, with catalogue-example.json
```

Key data models (`src/data/models.ts`): `Artist` (id, name, optional imageUrl, `sound` weights per `SoundTag`; `sound` may be empty), `TasteProfile` (artistId -> 0..1), `Clip` (artist, festival, title, duration, bpm, optional `source`: YouTube video id, start/end seconds, channel, optional `vertical`), `Festival` (lineup in billing order, days, stages, optional `sets`, `posterImageUrl`, `startDate`/`endDate`, `notes`), `SetTime`/`FestivalSet` (artist, day, stage, start/end 24h), `FestivalLineup` (festival id, artist ids, optional sets/days/stages), `ArtistSimilarity` (pair, score 0..1), `CatalogSnapshot` (artists, festivals, clips, optional lineups and similarities), `CatalogSource` (`load(): Promise<CatalogSnapshot>`). Reactions: `{ liked, saved, skipped, watched? }` per clip id (`watched`: `quick` | `through` | `replayed`).

Pure logic (layout, taste engine) stays free of React so it can be tested and later moved server-side.

## Rules for changes

- Work in **small, testable milestones**. Propose the smallest next step before large changes. Stop after each milestone so the user can test on a real phone.
- **Never rebuild or redesign working features** unless explicitly asked. Extend, don't replace.
- No backend, auth, scraping, payments or AI services unless the user asks.
- Add dependencies only with `npx expo install` and only if they run in Expo Go.
- Real data comes from the user, through the catalogue file (`catalogue/afterglow.json` or their hosted file). Never invent lineups, set times or video sources; enter only what the user provides, from official sources. Don't add artist photos or festival posters yourself (the user supplies links they have rights to). `npm test` must stay clean for `catalogue/afterglow.json`.
- Respect reduced motion (`useReducedMotion`) and accessibility labels.
