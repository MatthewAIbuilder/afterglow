# Look after yourself in Spanish: lines for a qualified reviewer

The Spanish for Look after yourself (`src/i18n/es/care.ts`, from `src/features/care/`) was translated on 2026-10-06 with care to keep every number, time, dose and warning exactly as in English. Like the English guide (still awaiting review against DanceSafe / TripSit, see `docs/app-store.md`), it needs a qualified reviewer who knows harm-reduction wording in Latin American Spanish before launch. These are the choices the translator was least sure of:

1. **Substance names.** Kept as written: "MDMA / molly", "GHB / G", "Speed (anfetamina)". Translated: Hongos (Latin American; "setas" in Spain), LSD / ácido, Ketamina, Cocaína, "Comestible de cannabis", "Óxido nitroso / globos". "Éxtasis" or the regional "tacha" may be clearer than "molly".
2. **How each is taken** (used in "{name}, {how}." and "Tiempos calculados {how}."): por bebida, por vía oral, si se fuma o vapea, si se come, en papel (cartón), por la nariz (avoids Spain-only "esnifar"), si se inhala. "Times are for {how}." is the least literal line.
3. **Phase names:** Empezando (Coming up), "Cerca del momento más fuerte", Más fuerte (Strongest), Pasando el efecto / Se pasa (wearing off), "Pasa del todo hacia" (Worn off by), "Ya debería haber pasado".
4. **"Sip water, about a cup an hour"** → "más o menos una taza por hora" ("taza" keeps the cup as the measure; "vaso" is more natural but can be bigger).
5. **"Overheating"** → "sobrecalentamiento". **"Feeling too high passes"** → "Si el efecto se siente demasiado fuerte, se pasa" (no neutral slang word for "high").
6. **"be sick"** → "vomitar" (ketamine + alcohol, the Sick tip); **"passing out"** → "perder el conocimiento"; **"faint"** → "sentir que te vas a desmayar".
7. **"a friend"** → mostly "alguien de confianza" (avoids gendered amigo/amiga); "a sober friend" → "alguien sobrio"; "you or a friend" → "tú o alguien de tu grupo". This slightly widens "friend".
8. **"medics"** → "personal médico"; **"welfare tents"** → "carpas de bienestar"; **"medical tent"** → "carpa médica"; **"chill-out area"** → "zona de descanso".
9. **Feeling names** (gender-neutral nouns): Genial, Bien, Normal (Okay), Cansancio, Ansiedad, Mucho calor, Agobio (Overwhelmed, a bit Spain-leaning), Malestar (Sick; loses the vomiting sense), No muy bien.
10. **"until 6 AM"** stays "hasta las 6 AM".
11. **Emergency numbers** come from the code unchanged ("Llamar al {number}").
12. **Check-in** → "chequeo" (matches Settings); **stopwatch** → "cronómetro".
13. **How you feel, redesigned** (added later on 2026-10-06): the groups "Algo no va bien" (Something’s off) and "Necesito ayuda" (Need a hand); "Registrado hace {time}" (Checked in … ago); "Avisar a mi grupo" (Tell my crew); and the recovery note "Sentiste {feeling} a las {time}, y desde entonces te has sentido mejor." (the feeling is the lower-case noun, e.g. "sentiste mucho calor"; "a las 1:05" should strictly be "a la 1:05" just after 1 AM). Only what the person said, never a guess at why.

A stopwatch keeps the name it was started with (saved on the phone), so one started in English stays English after switching to Spanish.
